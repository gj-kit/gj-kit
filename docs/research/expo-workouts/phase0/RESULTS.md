# Phase 0 — measured facts (2026-08-22)

> Three parallel lanes (`ios-a`, `ios-b`, `android-build`+`android-core`, `android-routes`) executed tests **T1–T8** and **T11** from §3 of the research index against real HealthKit and Health Connect. This document is the fact appendix; the index's §7 links back to it.
>
> Facts continue the index's numbering from 60. Every fact carries a confidence tag and the raw log that supports it. Log paths are relative to `docs/research/expo-workouts/phase0/logs/`.
>
> **Tags.** `[sim-test]` = executed on the iOS 26.5 simulator (`iPhone 17` / `iPhone 17 Pro`). `[device-test]` = executed on the Android emulator `emulator-5556` (AVD `Pixel_9a_hcprobe`, API 36, HealthFitness APEX `360526040`, controller versionName `16`). `[unverified]` = a lane reported it but the evidence in the raw log does not carry the claim, or the case could not be reached at all.
>
> **Everything here is simulator/emulator.** No physical iPhone and no physical Android device were available. Absolute timings are host-CPU figures; byte-level constants are this platform build's parcel encoding.

## 1. iOS — the route write path (T1)

62. **Path A′ throws on a write that already succeeded.** With the same shape plus an explicit `finishRoute(with:)` after `finishWorkout()`, the route was **already** associated before the call (`assocRouteCount = 1`, `seriesCount = 70`, after 2 ms) and the explicit call then threw `Error Domain=com.apple.healthkit Code=3 "This route builder is attached to a workout builder and will be finished with the workout builder"` (`HKErrorClass=HDWorkoutRouteBuilderServer`, `HKErrorSelector=_queue_finishRouteWithWorkout:metadata:completion:`). No double-write, no data loss — a spurious error any implementation would have to special-case by message string. `[sim-test]` — `ios-a-T1T2-main-probe.txt` (`T1.Ap.*`) · closes T1
63. **Path B works cleanly and preserves route metadata.** A direct `HKWorkoutRouteBuilder(healthStore:device: nil)` against an already-saved workout, 80 points, `finishRoute(with:metadata:)`: no error, `uuid=3F321914 seriesCount=80`, associated after 3 ms, 80/80 streamed, and `route.metadata` preserved `{HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier, HKMetadataKeySyncVersion: 1}`. 64. **A single insert error silently poisons a series builder.** `insertRouteData` **before** `beginCollection` threw `HKError Code=100 "Unable to associate series to workout builder."` — and after that error the two subsequent inserts (30 points during collection, 30 after `endCollection`) both returned `ok`, `finishWorkout()` returned a workout, and `assocRouteCount` stayed **0** after 6 278 ms of polling with **no orphan created**. 60 accepted points vanished with no error at finish. ## 3. iOS — route read cost and chunk size (T3)

77. **Neither route-read API leaks.** 50 iterations × 5 read strategies (310 full reads of a **36 000**-point route) in one process: `whole_descriptor` `delta i1→i50 = −11.03 MB` (no growth, oscillation only), `chunk250 +0.16`, `chunk500 +0.08`, `chunk1000 +0.08`, `chunk2000 +0.12`, `whole_legacyQuery` (kingstinct-style `HKWorkoutRouteQuery` callback) `+0.14 MB`. Process footprint after all 310 reads = 22.8 MB, still 22.9 MB after 20 s idle. Each mode reproduced in its own cold process. `[sim-test]` — `ios-b-T3-read-50x-36k.txt`, `ios-b-T3-isolated-modes.txt` · closes T3
78. **Peak memory is linear at ~415 bytes per point held.** Cold process, arrays pinned: baseline 17.69 MB → **29.72 MB** with `[CLLocation]` alive (+12.03) → **31.94 MB** with `[CLLocation]` + converted structs alive (+14.25) = 415–416 B/point. Chunked peaks over the same 36 000 points: `chunk250 +0.28 MB`, `chunk500 +0.31`, **`chunk1000 +0.55`**, `chunk2000 +0.97`. 1000-point chunks cost **26× less peak memory** than the whole-array read. `[sim-test]` — `ios-b-T3-memory-cold-isolated.txt`, `ios-b-T3-marshalling-cost.txt` · closes T3
79. **Chunking is free in wall clock; read cost is ~31 µs/point of unavoidable IPC.** Means over 50 iterations of a full 36 000-point read: `whole_descriptor` 1208 ms (median 1167), `chunk250` 1117, `chunk500` 1109, `chunk1000` 1118, `chunk2000` 1117, `legacy` 1116 — all within noise. Scaling (median of 5): 10 pts = 1 ms, 1 000 = 33 ms, 3 600 = 113 ms, 10 000 = 311 ms, 36 000 = 1 100 ms ⇒ **30.6–33.0 µs/point** with essentially zero fixed per-query overhead. `[sim-test]` — `ios-b-T3-read-50x-36k.txt`, `ios-b-T3-scaling.txt` · closes T3
80. **Struct marshalling is 0.4 % of the cost.** Building the 36 000-element array of plain Swift structs from `[CLLocation]` takes **4.55 ms** first pass, 3.91–4.43 ms over 5 repeats = **0.126 µs/point**. Collecting locations *without* building structs still takes 1112 ms. ~99.6 % of a route read is HealthKit IPC delivery. `[sim-test]` — `ios-b-T3-marshalling-cost.txt`, `ios-b-T3-read-50x-36k.txt` (`whole_locationsOnly`) · closes T3
   *Not measured:* the JS-bridge crossing cost. The probe is a pure Swift app with no Expo module, so the chunk-size recommendation rests on native memory and native wall clock only.

## 5. iOS — the time window (T11)

87. **`.strictStartDate` is exactly "start instant in `[from, to)`", and the default is overlap.** A workout `2026-08-19 23:50 +09:00 → 2026-08-20 00:10 +09:00`, queried with a sync-identifier predicate so nothing else can pollute the result:
    - `[day1, day2)` `.strictStartDate` → **1** · `[day2, day3)` `.strictStartDate` → **0**
    - `[day1, day2)` **no options** → 1 · `[day2, day3)` **no options** → **1** ← the same workout returned by two consecutive day windows
    - `.strictEndDate`: day1 → 0, day2 → 1
    - `.strictStartDate + .strictEndDate`: day1 → **0**, day2 → **0** ← a midnight-crossing workout belongs to no day at all
    - `[start, day3)` `.strictStartDate` → 1 (lower bound **inclusive**) · `[day1, start)` `.strictStartDate` → 0 (upper bound **exclusive**)

    `[sim-test]` — `ios-b-T11-single-window-rule.txt` · closes T11 (iOS half)
    *Caveat:* single local timezone (Asia/Seoul, UTC+9, no DST). A DST transition was not exercised.

## 7. Android — upsert and delete semantics (T6)

92. **The UUID is deterministic and is not freed by deletion.** All five upserts of `clientRecordId "t6#session"` returned `recordIdsList = [0ee91e13-fe90-3ebb-9b17-fb5725d6ca40]` — a v3 (MD5 name-based) UUID — identical across update, across delete, and across **re-insert after delete**. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` · closes T6
93. **Equal version overwrites; a lower version is a silent no-op.** Phase 2 (`version 1`, equal, different title/end, 12-point route) overwrote: same UUID, `clientRecordVersion` still 1, `lastModifiedTime` advanced `…21.297Z → …21.353Z`, title `"T6 v1-EQUAL"`, route 12 points. Phase 3 (`version 0`, lower) → `insertRecords` returned **normally**, `ok = true`, `recordIdsList = [0ee91e13-…]` — the *same UUID as if it had written* — and the read-back was byte-identical to phase 2 (`lastModifiedTime` unchanged). No exception, no warning, no distinguishable return value. Phase 4 (`version 2`, higher) overwrote. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`upsertPhases[1..3]`) · closes T6
94. **The stale-version no-op still emits an `UpsertionChange` — the Changes API cannot detect it either.** The drain immediately after phase 3 returned exactly **1** `UpsertionChange` whose payload was the **unchanged** phase-2 record (`clientRecordVersion 1`, `lastModifiedTime …21.353Z`, title `"T6 v1-EQUAL"`). Control: the drain taken after only failed deletes returned `count = 0`, so drains are exhaustive and the phase-3 change row was genuinely new. **The only reliable stale-version detection is a read-back comparing `clientRecordVersion` / `lastModifiedTime`.** `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`upsertPhases[2].changes`, `deleteMissingChanges.count = 0`) · closes T6 (new — not in the research pack)
95. **An upsert without a route destroys the stored route.** Phase 5 (`version 3`, no `exerciseRoute`, writer holding `WRITE_EXERCISE_ROUTE`): `exerciseRouteResult` became `NoData` (was `Data(20)`). Confirms index f43 on device. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`upsertPhases[4]`) · closes T6
96. **Unknown delete ids are silent; malformed ids are a hard error.** `deleteRecords(clientRecordIdsList = ["definitely-missing-id"])` did not throw and emitted **zero** change-log entries; a well-formed but non-existent UUID did not throw; deleting the same `clientRecordId` twice did not throw. But `recordIdsList = ["not-a-uuid-at-all"]` **threw** `java.lang.IllegalArgumentException` caused by `android.health.connect.HealthConnectException` with `errorCode = 3 (ERROR_INVALID_ARGUMENT)`, message `java.lang.NumberFormatException: Error at index 0 in: "not"`. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`deleteMissing.*`, `repeatDeleteSameId`) · closes T6
97. **`DeletionChange` carries only `recordId`.** Reflection on the change object gave `declaredFields = [recordId]` — no record type, no `clientRecordId`, no `dataOrigin`. Deleting the real record emitted exactly 1 `DeletionChange`. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`realDeleteChanges`) · closes T6, confirms index f38
98. **Metric records do not cascade on session delete; the route does.** `DistanceRecord` (5000 m), `ActiveCaloriesBurnedRecord` (310 kcal), `ElevationGainedRecord` (42 m), `StepsRecord` (6123) and `HeartRateRecord` (10 samples) were written over the same window with derived `clientRecordId`s `t6#session:{distance,kcal,elev,steps,hr}`. Before the session delete: 1 of each. **After**: still 1 of each, same `clientRecordId`s. `[device-test]` — `30-t6-upsert-result.json` (`metrics.beforeSessionDelete` / `afterSessionDelete`) · closes T6

## 8. Android — route size ceilings (T4)

99. **The record-size ceiling is exact and linear.** Every failure reports `java.lang.IllegalStateException` caused by `android.health.connect.HealthConnectException` `errorCode = 7` with `Record size exceeded the single record size limit: 1000000, was: N`. Exact pin: **20 828 OK · 20 829 FAIL (1 000 004 B) · 20 830 FAIL (1 000 052 B)**. ## 9. Android — the time window (T11)

## Decisions Phase 0 forces on the design

**iOS write path**

- The iOS route write **MUST** use **path B**: `HKWorkoutBuilder` → `beginCollection` → `addSamples` → `addMetadata(syncId/syncVersion)` → `endCollection` → `finishWorkout()`; then, only if the returned workout is non-nil, a **separate** `HKWorkoutRouteBuilder(healthStore:device: nil)` → chunked `insertRouteData` → `finishRoute(with: savedWorkout, metadata:)`. Because a poisoned series builder silently yields a route-less workout with no error at finish (f64), `discard()` on a series builder destroys the workout save (f65), and only path B can attach a route to an already-saved workout (f66).
- **NEVER** call `workoutBuilder.seriesBuilder(for:)`, and never call `discard()` on a builder obtained from one.
- Route sync identifiers **MUST** be derived per workout (`"<workoutSyncId>/route"`), never shared across workouts, because a replacement route inherits the replaced route's workout association and cross-links both (f68).
- Treat **any** `insertRouteData` error as fatal for that route builder and surface it as a typed error — never keep inserting (f64).
- Reject an empty point list with our own typed error before calling `finishRoute`, which otherwise throws `HKError 3 "No data was added to the workout route."` (f84).
- Make `finishRoute` retryable by constructing a **fresh** builder — a repeat call on the same builder throws `HKError 3 "Workout route already finished."` (f63).

**iOS totals and model**

- Read totals as: `statistics(for:)` → *(defensive, currently unreachable)* deprecated totals → time-window `HKStatisticsQueryDescriptor` with `.strictStartDate`. Keep tier 2 in the code for older OS versions even though iOS 26.5 never reaches it (f71, f73).
- Tag tier 1 as provenance **`associated`** only if a `HKQuery.predicateForObjects(from: workout)` sample query for that quantity type returns > 0 samples; otherwise it is a synthesised legacy total and **MUST** be tagged `total` (f71).
- Use `workout.duration` for `activeDurationSec` and `endDate − startDate` for `wallClockSec` (f75).
- For D11 `indoor`, read `metadata["HKIndoorWorkout"]` first and fall back to `workoutActivities[0].workoutConfiguration.locationType == .indoor`; report `indoor` as **undefined**, not `false`, when the key is absent and `locationType` is 3 (f76).

**iOS route read**

- `getRoute()` **MUST** iterate `HKWorkoutRouteQueryDescriptor(route).results(for:)` and convert-and-release every **1000** points; never accumulate a full `[CLLocation]` (f78).
- Run it on the **Swift-concurrency** `AsyncFunction` overload, never the closure-style serial queue — budget ~31 µs/point (a 1-hour route ≈ 113 ms, a 10-hour route ≈ 1.1 s) (f79, index f8).
- Ship **no** defensive per-call teardown, `autoreleasepool` gymnastics or health-store recycling: neither route-read API leaks (f77). kingstinct #370 is a bug in their retain graph.

**Route hygiene (both platforms, owned by our native layer)**

- Before writing on iOS, our layer **MUST** drop `horizontalAccuracy < 0` or `> 50 m`, decide explicitly about points outside `[workout.start, workout.end)`, and reject/clamp `lat ∉ [−90,90]` / `lon ∉ [−180,180]` — HealthKit filters none of it while Health Connect throws (f81, f85).
- Our layer **MUST** de-duplicate route points by timestamp itself, **keeping the last occurrence**, so iOS (silent dedupe) and Android (throws) agree (f82).
- On read, map negative accuracy/speed/course sentinels to `undefined` in `RoutePoint` rather than surfacing `−1` to JS (f83).

**Time windows**

- Every time window on both platforms means **"start instant in `[from, to)`"**. iOS: always `HKQuery.predicateForSamples(withStart:end:options: .strictStartDate)` — never the default (overlap double-counts a midnight-crossing workout across two day windows) and never `.strictStartDate + .strictEndDate` (such a workout then belongs to no day) (f87). Android: always `TimeRangeFilter.between(Instant, Instant)` (f107).
- **NEVER** expose or use Health Connect's `LocalDateTime` overloads, and never let a caller-supplied "local day" reach the filter — the local column is the record's *own* stored offset (f108). The JS surface takes epoch-ms only; day bucketing is the consuming app's job, done afterwards from `utcOffsetMin`.

**Android write**

- `saveWorkout` **MUST** be full-state — always send the route when the workout has one, or the upsert deletes the stored route (f95).
- Derive `clientRecordVersion` from the workout's own `updatedAt` so equal-version clobbering of newer data cannot happen (f93).
- A stale-version write is undetectable from `insertRecords` **and** from the Changes API (f93, f94). Either pay for a read-back that compares `clientRecordVersion`/`lastModifiedTime` (make it an opt-in `verifyWrite` path) or **document** that a lower version silently does nothing. Do not pretend to report `staleVersion` without the read-back.
- Ship a write-side `routeTooLarge` guard **in Kotlin before the platform call**, using `estimatedRecordBytes = 160 + 48·points + 2·(title+notes+clientRecordId lengths) + 24·(segments+laps)`, throwing `ERR_WORKOUTS_ROUTE_TOO_LARGE` at a **safe threshold of 20 000 points** (960 160 B bare, ~40 KB headroom under the 1 000 000 B ceiling). Do **not** budget separately for bare vs full-field routes — the optional fields are free (f99, f100).
- Map `IllegalStateException(cause = HealthConnectException(errorCode 7))` whose message contains `single record size limit` to `ERR_WORKOUTS_ROUTE_TOO_LARGE`, **not** to a rate-limit error (f101).

**Android delete**

- `deleteWorkout` by `clientRecordId` **MUST** map "unknown id" to success, never to an error (f96) — but **MUST** validate that any raw platform id from JS parses as a UUID first, because a malformed id throws `errorCode 3` → `ERR_WORKOUTS_INVALID_ARGUMENT` (f96).
- `deleteWorkout` **MUST** also delete the metric records explicitly, one `deleteRecords` call per type, using deterministic derived `clientRecordId`s (`"${workoutId}:distance"` etc., verified to work) — they do not cascade (f98).

**Android sync**

- The incremental-sync layer **MUST** filter change logs by `dataOrigin.packageName` and **MUST NOT** treat an `UpsertionChange` as proof anything changed (f94).
- Key the local store by **platform UUID**: `DeletionChange` carries only a `recordId` (f97). The module can reconstruct its *own* ids from the deterministic name-based UUID (f92), but cannot resolve a foreign app's deletion to a record type.

**Android routes and permissions**

- `routeState` maps 1:1 to the platform tri-state: `Data → available`, `NoData → none`, `ConsentRequired → consentRequired`. **NEVER** collapse `ConsentRequired` to `none` — it means "there IS a route, you just cannot see it" (f114, f118).
- `requestPermissions()` **MUST NOT** put `READ_EXERCISE_ROUTES` in the contract set as a way of asking for it, and **MUST NOT** infer denial from its absence in the result. Model it as a separate non-requestable scope whose only truthful read is `getGrantedPermissions().contains(...)` (f110). Declaring it in the manifest is mandatory — undeclared silently yields `null` (f112).
- `requestPermissions()` **MUST** have **no internal timeout** (onboarding + additional-access screens took 41.6 s of scripted tapping) and **MUST** treat an empty returned set as **`inconclusive`**, never `denied` — the user may have bounced off the onboarding screen (f120, f122). Distinguish real denial only by comparing before/after `getGrantedPermissions()`.
- `getRoute(foreignSessionId)` **MUST** map every `null` to **one** opaque outcome (`ERR_WORKOUTS_ROUTE_UNAVAILABLE`) and document that the cause is unknowable — deny / no-route / bad-id / unknown-id / undeclared are byte-identical (f112). Do not invent a `denied` code.
- `getRoute()` **MUST** wrap the `ExerciseRouteRequestContract` launcher in a **timeout** (10 s; every success returned in < 200 ms) because the callback provably never fires on Intent overflow (f104), and **MUST NOT** fan out route requests in a loop — a 20 k-point request crashes the Health Connect UI process and takes the calling Activity with it (f105).
- Validate the session id before launching: an empty string throws client-side (`ERR_WORKOUTS_INVALID_ARGUMENT`) (f112).
- Refuse (or short-circuit to `consentRequired`) any route read initiated while `RunningAppProcessInfo.importance > IMPORTANCE_FOREGROUND`, and state in the README that `READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` does **not** help (f113).
- Recompute `routeState` on **every** read and never cache it across app sessions — an app can lose access to routes it wrote itself (f114). Present `WRITE_EXERCISE_ROUTE` as a **read-affecting** scope.
- Page conservatively (50–100 sessions, not the 1000 default) whenever route permission is held, and feed the **already-materialised** routes straight into the native route cache instead of re-reading them for `getRoute()` — the inline materialisation is a 12×–19× cost with no opt-out (f116).

**Android platform integration**

- `openSettings()` **MUST** use `android.health.connect.action.HEALTH_HOME_SETTINGS`, with `MANAGE_HEALTH_PERMISSIONS` + `android.intent.extra.PACKAGE_NAME` as the per-app variant. **NEVER** use `androidx.health.ACTION_HEALTH_CONNECT_SETTINGS` on API 34+ — it does not resolve and would throw `ActivityNotFoundException` (f119). Guard every `startActivity` with a resolve check.
- `openStoreListing()` ships the `market://` URI unchanged plus the `https://play.google.com` fallback (f119).
- The config plugin **MUST** register an `activity-alias` for `android.intent.action.VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE` + category `android.intent.category.HEALTH_PERMISSIONS`, protected by `android:permission="android.permission.START_VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE"` — that is what the Android 14+ UI launches. Keep the `androidx.health.ACTION_SHOW_PERMISSIONS_RATIONALE` activity for the API 28–33 path but do not expect it to fire on 34+ (f123).
- The availability check **MUST** use `getSdkStatus(context)` only and never touch `PackageManager` (f88). It should additionally treat "sessions readable but every foreign route `ConsentRequired`" as a plausible **not-onboarded** state and point the user at `openSettings()` (f115).
- **Do not build the metric read path on `aggregate()` alone.** Treat a `null` aggregate as **unknown** (surface `undefined`, never `0 kcal` / `0 m`) and ship a `readRecords` + client-side-sum fallback filtered by `dataOrigin` (f109).

**E2E automation**

- The Android smoke flow: optional `text: "Get started"` (onboarding, first run only) → `text: "Allow all"` → `text: "Allow"` → optional `text: "Allow"` again (additional-access screen) → for routes `text: "Allow all routes"`. Every extra screen must be `optional: true`, and the flow must tolerate label drift (f120–f124).
- The iOS smoke flow must boot the simulator **windowed** (`open -a Simulator`) and pass `-parallel-testing-enabled NO`, or it will hang or read a stale container (f125, f126).

## Still open / must be designed defensively

- **`pendingUnlock` (T1 sub-case) — never reproduced.** No simulator mechanism exists to force protected-data-unavailable and no physical iPhone was available (f70). **Design assumption:** treat `finishWorkout()` returning `(nil workout, nil error)` as a **third** outcome distinct from success and failure. The retry must re-query the workout by `HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier` and, if found, attach the route to the **re-fetched** workout with a **fresh** direct `HKWorkoutRouteBuilder` (proven to work, f66). Assume `finishRoute` itself can fail with `errorDatabaseInaccessible` while locked and make it retryable. Positive consequence of choosing path B: because we never call `finishRoute` unless a saved workout exists, a locked device can never produce an orphaned route on our write path.
- **Health Connect `aggregate()` returns null for everything (T11 half) — root cause unknown.** Reproduced before *and* after onboarding, so the app-priority-list hypothesis is dead (f109). Until someone reproduces working aggregates on a Play-updated device, the `readRecords` + client-side-sum fallback is **mandatory**, and null must mean unknown. The "aggregate double-counts a midnight session in both days" claim remains source-only.
- **API 28–33 (the closed-source Health Connect APK path) was never tested.** No such system image is installed and downloads were out of bounds. Every T4/T5/T6/T8/T11 Android fact is an Android 14+ APEX-path fact. In particular `androidx.health.ACTION_HEALTH_CONNECT_SETTINGS` failing to resolve is an API-36 observation; it is presumably the *correct* action on the APK path, so `openSettings()` needs a version-branched implementation that was never exercised. D6's "best effort" tier still rests on KDoc alone.
- **Byte-level constants are one platform build's parcel encoding.** 48 B/route-point on insert, 60 B/point on the Intent path, 24 B/segment, 24 B/lap, and the ~512 KiB Intent boundary were measured on HealthFitness APEX **360526040** (the *factory* copy; the original research measured Pixel_9a with an updated **360915160**). A Mainline update can change them. Treat the formula as a **safety margin, not a contract**, and keep the guard well below the ceiling. The Intent cut is bracketed (511 020 B OK / 524 220 B FAIL) and is probably not deterministic — the 1 MB Binder buffer is process-wide, so a busier process would fail lower.
- **The per-route CONSENT-DIALOG path was only exercised with a 25-point route.** Whether it carries the same ~512 KiB Intent ceiling and the same never-fires-callback failure mode is untested. Keep the timeout regardless (f111, f104).
- **`FLAG_PERMISSION_USER_FIXED` was never reached.** The route dialog was denied only once and the flag did not appear. AOSP says a `USER_FIXED` `READ_EXERCISE_ROUTES` makes `RouteRequestActivity` cancel **silently forever** — a permanent, invisible "routes never work" state. How many denials trigger it, and whether Health Connect Settings can recover it, is unknown.
- **The Health Connect 30-day history wall was never crossed against the route dialog.** Every fixture is within ~6 days, so `isSessionInaccessible` (which also silently cancels the dialog) was never exercised.
- **`com.android.healthconnect.flags.read_exercise_routes_all_enabled` could not be read** — `adb shell aflags list` needs root and `adb root` is refused on a `google_apis_playstore` image. Moot for our conclusion (foreign routes do work) but it cannot be ruled in or out as a variable on other devices.
- **Health Connect onboarding is now COMPLETED on emulator-5556 and cannot be undone without wiping the AVD.** Any lane that wants to re-observe the not-onboarded behaviour must create a new AVD. Conversely, **every Phase-0 measurement taken before that point was taken on a not-onboarded device** and should be re-checked if it involved routes.
- **The JS-bridge crossing cost for a route chunk was not measured** (pure Swift probe, no Expo module). If Phase 1 finds the bridge crossing expensive, 1000 can be raised to 2000 for +0.42 MB peak with no other penalty (f78, f79).
- **Fitness.app was never reachable** (personal-details onboarding), so the DTS "out-of-window routes do not render in Fitness" claim is refuted for **Health.app only** (f86).
- **Single timezone, no DST transition** on either platform's T11 (Asia/Seoul, UTC+9).
- **The 5 MB per-request chunk cap** (`RateLimiter__max_write_chunk_size=5000000`) was read from `device_config` but never exercised with a multi-record insert. A `saveWorkout` writing session + 5 metric records is nowhere near it; a caller batching many workouts could hit it.
- **T9 (packaging / `app.plugin.js` under `"type": "module"`) and the iOS half of T10 (HealthKit sheet `maestro hierarchy`) were not run** and remain open from §3 of the index.
- **Test data was deliberately LEFT on emulator-5556** for later lanes: 17 `com.gjkit.hcwriter` sessions `t4-full-{100,…,20828}`, each carrying a route of exactly that many points, spread over the last ~6 days — a ready-made foreign-app route fixture set. **Requesting the 15 000- and 20 000-point ones through `ExerciseRouteRequestContract` crashes the Health Connect controller process** (f105). Clean up with `cd docs/research/expo-workouts/phase0/hcprobe && ./run2.sh writer t4wipe 60 <logbase>`.
